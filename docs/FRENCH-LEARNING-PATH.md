# French learning path and conversation passport

Updated 2026-11-16. This is an original practice curriculum, not a validated placement test or fluency qualification. Every chapter is explorable; the next-step recommendation follows the order below.

## A visit in four steps

1. Meet a few illustrated cards. Listen, recall, turn the card and reflect.
2. Complete a text or mixed lesson practice. Pause any time.
3. Say a model phrase with a trusted grown-up; microphone use is optional.
4. Revisit earlier words or try the chapter conversation mission.

Choose 5/10/15 minutes and 2/3/5/7 visits weekly. These preferences persist per learner, but do not impose a deadline.

## Chapter 1: Hello and introductions

Start a friendly conversation with a greeting, a name, and a simple feeling.

**Conversation goal:** Say hello, share a fictional name, and ask how someone feels.

| Step | Lesson | Practice aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sound and hello | Use a greeting and a polite first exchange. |
| 2 | My name | Introduce yourself and ask someone’s name. |
| 3 | How I feel | Describe a feeling and ask how someone is doing. |
| 4 | Letters and accents | Explore all 26 French letter names and notice the marks that change spelling or sound. |
| 5 | Family and friends | Name relatives and talk about people who matter to you. |
| 6 | At school | Talk about common school things and activities. |

**Mission: Say hello and meet someone.** Two fictional classmates meet before an activity. Greet someone, share a fictional name, ask how they feel, and say goodbye.

- Greet someone and say your name.
- Ask how someone feels.
- Close a short conversation politely.

**Try away from the screen:** Practise a two-line greeting with a toy or imaginary character.

## Chapter 2: People, school and everyday life

Use short phrases to describe familiar people and everyday choices.

**Conversation goal:** Talk about familiar people, school, food, and simple routines.

| Step | Lesson | Practice aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | Food I like | Name foods and say what you like to eat. |
| 8 | A, the and little nouns | Meet un, une, le, la and les with everyday objects. |
| 9 | Having and being | Use common forms of avoir and être to describe people and things. |
| 10 | Ask a question | Ask simple questions with est-ce que, où, quand and pourquoi. |
| 11 | Days and dates | Name the days of the week and ask what day it is. |
| 12 | Count to twenty | Recognise and use the numbers zero to twenty. |

**Mission: Talk about a school day.** Two fictional classmates chat about a school garden. Ask about a familiar activity and respond with a simple detail.

- Name a family member or friend.
- Say what you like to eat.
- Describe a simple school day.

**Try away from the screen:** Name three things you might see in a classroom and say them in French.

## Chapter 3: Ask questions and find things

Find a place or object by asking and listening for useful details.

**Conversation goal:** Ask clear questions, describe objects, and follow simple directions.

| Step | Lesson | Practice aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13 | Colors around us | Name familiar colors and agree the color word with its noun. |
| 14 | Actions with -er verbs | Use a regular -er verb in the present tense. |
| 15 | Not and never | Make a simple negative with ne…pas and recognise ne…jamais. |
| 16 | Nouns and agreement | Notice masculine and feminine nouns and match common adjectives. |
| 17 | Where things are | Describe where familiar objects are using simple place words. |
| 18 | Find the way | Understand simple directions and ask where a place is. |

**Mission: Find the library.** A fictional visitor asks a local student for directions. Ask where a place is, listen, and check the route.

- Ask where something is.
- Use a simple negative.
- Follow a short direction.

**Try away from the screen:** Give an imaginary toy one French direction: à gauche, à droite, or tout droit.

## Chapter 4: Daily life and plans

Make a simple plan and explain what you need for the day.

**Conversation goal:** Talk about time, weather, shopping, routines, and tomorrow.

| Step | Lesson | Practice aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 19 | The time of day | Talk about a daily schedule and ask the time. |
| 20 | Weather report | Describe simple weather and choose clothes for the day. |
| 21 | At the shop | Ask for an item, its price and a polite amount. |
| 22 | My routine | Describe a familiar daily routine with time words. |
| 23 | Plans for tomorrow | Make a near-future plan with aller + infinitive. |
| 24 | Join the ideas | Connect thoughts with and, but, because, so and then. |

**Mission: Order a snack.** A fictional student orders a snack at a market stall. Make a polite request, understand a price, and finish the exchange.

- Say what the weather is like.
- Ask for something politely.
- Describe a plan for tomorrow.

**Try away from the screen:** Choose tomorrow’s weather and say one item you would take.

## Chapter 5: Stories and choices

Tell what happened and connect ideas with useful time words.

**Conversation goal:** Describe past events, compare options, and share a reason.

| Step | Lesson | Practice aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 | A finished event | Say what someone did using a common passé composé form with avoir. |
| 26 | Setting a scene | Recognise the imparfait for a background or repeated past action. |
| 27 | Tell a short story | Put events in order and keep the listener oriented. |
| 28 | Opinions and reasons | Share a simple opinion and explain why. |
| 29 | Compare choices | Describe a helpful feature and a drawback without calling either choice best for everyone. |
| 30 | Maybe and if | Talk about a possibility and a simple condition. |

**Mission: Tell a park story.** A fictional friend tells what happened on a walk. Describe the setting, a past event, and what happened next.

- Say what happened yesterday.
- Give a reason for a choice.
- Put events in order.

**Try away from the screen:** Tell a tiny three-part story using d’abord, ensuite, and finalement.

## Chapter 6: Keep a conversation going

Respond flexibly when a plan changes or a word is missing.

**Conversation goal:** Repair a misunderstanding, negotiate plans, and tell a connected story.

| Step | Lesson | Practice aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 31 | When a word is missing | Ask for repetition, clarification or a slower explanation. |
| 32 | Make and adjust a plan | Suggest a meeting plan and negotiate a change politely. |
| 33 | Talk about a shared interest | Ask what someone thinks and respond with agreement or a different view. |
| 34 | Ask for directions naturally | Explain where you are going and check you understood the route. |
| 35 | Solve a small shop problem | Ask about an item and handle a simple mismatch politely. |
| 36 | Tell a connected past story | Describe the setting, the main event and what happened afterward. |

**Mission: Change the plan.** Two fictional friends adjust a meeting when a place is closed. Suggest another place, agree on a time, and confirm the plan.

- Ask someone to repeat.
- Suggest and adjust a plan.
- Share a connected past story.

**Try away from the screen:** Role-play changing a pretend meeting time and agree on a new one.

## Achievement rules

- First lesson: a lesson at 70% or above on first answers; helped answers earn no independent credit.
- First conversation: an independently completed guided mission at 70%+, all turns answered, no help.
- Vocabulary review: reflect on at least five picture cards. This celebrates review, not proven recall.
- Six separate chapter stamps: each requires its six lesson goals and its own passed mission.
- Final passport: all six chapter stamps. Repeating a mission does not generate duplicate badges.

Stamps measure performance in these guided exercises. They do not certify spontaneous conversation, pronunciation, fluency, intelligence or a CEFR level.

## Resume and identity

One current activity checkpoint is auto-saved for each learner. It preserves question/card number, original responses, retry feedback, typed text, sentence tokens and help state. Switching learners/accounts keeps their checkpoints separate. Starting a different activity asks before replacing the active checkpoint. Completing or deleting an activity clears it.

The data lives on this device, separated by verified adult account or guest. Google/email activation remains incomplete. No child email/name is requested, no audio is persisted, and there is no cross-device sync. Existing completed French results migrate automatically. Unfinished typed responses are not included in progress exports.
